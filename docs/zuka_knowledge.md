# ZUKA SOC Platform Knowledge Base & Operating Procedures

This document serves as the ground truth operational reference for the Zuka Operations Assistant. Every response regarding platform modules, operational workflows, and cybersecurity terms must strictly derive from this document.

---

## 1. Platform Modules & Departments

### A. Alert Center
- **Function**: Ingests, inspects, and manages real-time network threat detections generated by AI and signature detection sensors.
- **Threat Classes**: Detects DoS, PortScan, BruteForce, Botnet, Infiltration, and WebAttacks.
- **Attributes**: Each alert contains `alert_id`, `detected_at`, `predicted_class`, `confidence` score (0-100%), `severity` (LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH, CRITICAL), `status` (OPEN, ACKNOWLEDGED), `source_ip`, `destination_ip`, `source_port`, `destination_port`, and `protocol`.
- **Explainable AI (XAI)**: Provides feature importance charts demonstrating why the ML model identified the traffic as malicious.
- **Action**: Analysts must review the threat and click **Acknowledge** (or ask Zuka: `acknowledge alert <ID>`).

### B. Incident Management
- **Function**: Tracks and manages active security incidents created from critical or confirmed alerts.
- **Lifecycle Statuses**:
  - `OPEN`: Newly created, unacknowledged incident.
  - `ACKNOWLEDGED`: Linked alert acknowledged; ready for investigation.
  - `INVESTIGATING`: Active forensic investigation in progress by an assigned analyst.
  - `RESOLVED`: Threat mitigated, root cause identified, resolution summary recorded (pre-filled by the analyst conclusion by default, which can be overridden by the analyst).
  - `CLOSED`: Post-mortem complete and officially archived.
- **Escalation Levels**: Level 0 (Normal) to Level 3 (Critical Management Escalation).
- **Priorities**: P1 (Urgent/Critical), P2 (High), P3 (Medium), P4 (Low).
- **Prerequisites**: "Resolve Incident" cannot be executed until the linked forensic investigation is marked `COMPLETED` with an analyst conclusion.

### C. Defense Department
- **Function**: Threat containment, network detection telemetry triage, and countermeasures deployment.
- **Scope**: Exclusively displays active **open investigations** (`IN_PROGRESS`).
- **Telemetry Identification**: Identifies attack class, AI confidence %, attacker source IP & port, targeted host & port, protocol, and automated threat diagnosis.
- **Containment Strategies**: Quarantine IoT/Device VLAN, Enforce Ingress Firewall Block on Attacker IP, Credential Rotation, Ingress Rate-Limiting, Firmware Lockdown, Custom Defense.
- **Synchronization**: Submitting a defense action automatically formats and appends the directive into the **Investigation Department** notes and logs an immutable audit event in `analyst_actions`.

### D. Investigation Department
- **Function**: Dedicated forensic workspace where analysts review packet data, record findings, and draw incident conclusions.
- **Prerequisite**: An investigation starts when the incident moves to `INVESTIGATING`.
- **Dossier**: Each investigation has a unique `investigation_id` (`INV-...`), status (`NOT_STARTED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED`), analyst notes (including defense directives), and manual conclusion.
- **Analyst Notes**: Timestamped records documenting forensic findings and defense directives.
- **Manual Conclusion**: SOC analyst manually writes the final verdict and marks status `COMPLETED` (with 0 blocking popup errors). This unlocks incident resolution.

### E. Network Monitor
- **Function**: Real-time packet telemetry visualizer.
- **Capabilities**: Displays live captured network flows, packet sizes, protocol breakdown (TCP, UDP, ICMP), port distributions, and traffic spikes.
- **Detection Feed**: Shows packet events feeding into detection workers.

### F. Database Center
- **Function**: Central operational ledger, historical data store, and database monitor.
- **Pure Observation Ledger**: Manual action entry removed; all analyst actions across the platform are automatically recorded in `analyst_actions`.
- **Capabilities**:
  - Live table health and row counts for alerts, incidents, investigations, and network events.
  - **Analyst Action Ledger**: Permanent log of human-authorized operational actions (who, what, when, action ID, and result).
  - **Audit History**: Security audit trail of user sessions, logins, and permission changes.

### G. Reports
- **Function**: Professional executive and compliance reporting.
- **Print Feature**: Isolated `@media print` styling guarantees clean document paper printing without dark website headers, sidebars, or toolbars.
- **Capabilities**: Generates multi-page PDF reports for specific incident IDs or alert IDs, including forensic summaries, network charts, and analyst sign-offs.

### H. User Management & Administration
- **Function**: Role-based access control (RBAC).
- **Roles**: `ADMIN` (Full privileges, user approval), `SOC_ANALYST` (Operational access to alerts, incidents, investigations, reports), `VIEWER` (Read-only access to dashboard and network monitor).
- **Admin Approval**: Newly created analyst accounts require admin approval before login.

## 2. Standard Operating Procedures & Workflows

### How do I acknowledge an alert?
1. Navigate to **Alert Center** (`/alerts`).
2. Click on the alert row to open the inspection drawer.
3. Review the threat classification, confidence, and source IP.
4. Click **Acknowledge Alert** (or tell Zuka: `acknowledge alert <ID>`).
5. Acknowledging unlocks the linked incident for investigation.

### How do I start an investigation?
1. Ensure the linked alert is acknowledged first.
2. In **Incident Management** (`/incidents`), click on the incident to inspect it.
3. Click **Start Investigation** (or tell Zuka: `investigate incident <ID>`).
4. The system opens an investigation dossier (`INV-...`), assigns the analyst, and updates status to `INVESTIGATING`.

### How do I add analyst notes?
1. Inside the investigation dossier or incident inspection panel, enter your notes in the analyst notes section.
2. Or tell Zuka: `add analyst note to <ID>: <your notes>`.
3. The note is permanently timestamped and logged with your analyst username and action ledger ID.

### How do I resolve an incident?
1. The incident's investigation **must be completed** with analyst notes and a conclusion first.
2. In Incident Management, click **Resolve Incident** (or tell Zuka: `resolve incident <ID> with summary: <summary>`).
3. Enter the resolution summary explaining how the threat was mitigated.
4. Status transitions to `RESOLVED` and `resolved_at` is stamped.

### How do I close an incident?
1. After an incident is `RESOLVED`, review the final post-mortem.
2. Click **Close Incident** (or tell Zuka: `close incident <ID>`).
3. Status transitions to `CLOSED` and `closed_at` is stamped.

### How do I generate a PDF report?
1. Navigate to **Reports** (`/reports`).
2. Select whether you want an **Incident Report** or **Alert Report**.
3. Choose the target ID from the dropdown or search bar.
4. Click **Generate PDF Report** to view or download.

## 3. Key Terminology

- **Alert**: A single suspicious network event detected by machine learning or intrusion detection rules.
- **Incident**: An aggregated operational security case that requires human triage and investigation.
- **Investigation**: The forensic process of analyzing packets, documenting findings, and reaching a conclusion.
- **Action Ledger ID**: A unique tracking identifier (e.g. `ACT-XXXXXXXX`) assigned to every human-executed action for compliance.
- **XAI (Explainable AI)**: Feature importance analysis demonstrating why a machine learning model flagged network packets as an attack.

## 4. Machine Learning, IoT Detection & Explainable AI (XAI)

### Model Architecture
- **Model Type**: Unified XGBoost Multiclass Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (`models/zuka_unified_xgboost/model.json`).
- **Dataset & Training**: Trained on 2,561,658 network flow records with balanced class weighting, achieving ~93.0% validation accuracy and 99.2% F1-score on DDoS detection.
- **13 Supported Classes**:
  1. `BENIGN`: Normal operational smart device traffic.
  2. `DOS`: Denial of Service attempting to exhaust device CPU/memory or network bandwidth.
  3. `DDOS`: Distributed Denial of Service with coordinated volumetric traffic targeting smart hubs.
  4. `PORT_SCAN`: Systematic TCP/UDP scanning probing smart devices for vulnerable open ports.
  5. `BRUTE_FORCE`: Automated credential guessing against default IoT logins (Telnet, SSH, HTTP admin).
  6. `BOTNET`: Mirai-style IoT botnet recruitment and Command & Control (C2) beaconing.
  7. `BACKDOOR`: Hidden listener or unauthorized reverse shell on a compromised smart device.
  8. `RECONNAISSANCE`: Host discovery and active network topology mapping.
  9. `EXPLOIT`: Known vulnerability payload directed against smart device firmware or services.
  10. `FUZZING`: High-entropy malformed packet inputs sent to crash embedded network buffers.
  11. `INFILTRATION`: Lateral movement from a compromised IoT peripheral into the internal network.
  12. `MALWARE`: Malicious binary download or communication payload targeting IoT platforms.
  13. `OTHER_ATTACK`: Anomalous security threats not fitting a single classic category.

### 14 Common Network Flow Features
1. `duration_sec`: Flow duration in seconds.
2. `src_packets`: Total packets transmitted by source.
3. `dst_packets`: Total packets transmitted by destination.
4. `src_bytes`: Total payload and header bytes sent by source.
5. `dst_bytes`: Total payload and header bytes sent by destination.
6. `flow_packet_rate`: Total packets per second in the flow.
7. `src_packet_rate`: Source transmission rate in packets per second.
8. `dst_packet_rate`: Destination transmission rate in packets per second.
9. `src_byte_rate`: Source data transfer rate in bytes per second.
10. `dst_byte_rate`: Destination data transfer rate in bytes per second.
11. `src_packet_size_mean`: Average size of packets sent by source.
12. `dst_packet_size_mean`: Average size of packets sent by destination.
13. `src_iat_mean_sec`: Mean inter-arrival time between source packets in seconds.
14. `dst_iat_mean_sec`: Mean inter-arrival time between destination packets in seconds.

### Explainable AI (TreeSHAP)
- **Engine**: TreeSHAP via `shap.TreeExplainer` on the trained XGBoost model.
- **Function**: Explains localized predictions by computing Shapley feature contribution values. Shows exactly which features (e.g. abnormal `flow_packet_rate`, miniature `src_iat_mean_sec`) drove the attack classification.
- **Transparency**: Allows analysts to audit model confidence and distinguish algorithmic inference from observed network facts.

## 5. Assistant Boundaries & Local Model Specification

- **Identity**: Zuka, an AI Cybersecurity Assistant designed specifically for smart-device network security and SOC triage.
- **Engine**: Local Ollama instance running `qwen2.5:3b`.
- **Zero Hallucination Constraint**: Never invents packet counts, timestamps, alert IDs, incident IDs, or device statuses. All metrics derive from live database records.
- **Safety Policy**: Strictly refuses requests to generate malware, craft exploit payloads, or conduct unauthorized attacks. All recommendations are purely defensive and educational.
